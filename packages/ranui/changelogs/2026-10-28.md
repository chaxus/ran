# Changelog — 2026-10-28

## packages/ranui — press feedback, rolling counts and stacked toasts

Three pieces of motion, measured against bencho.dev and held to the Motion rules in
`design/DESIGN.md`: only `transform`-family properties and `opacity` move, feedback lands
within 100ms, entering decelerates without overshoot, and reduced motion moves nothing.

### `r-button`: press within feedback time

- The press shrinks the content through the individual `scale` property, so a transform a
  host sets on it survives the press. **Breaking:** `--ran-btn-active-transform` is now
  `--ran-btn-active-scale` (default `0.96`), and a custom `--ran-btn-transition` has to name
  `scale` rather than `transform`.
- Press and release take 100ms on the standard curve, without the spring's overshoot.
- The click ripple keeps its 300ms spread and 800ms fade. It is decoration rather than
  feedback; at 100ms and 200ms it landed as a hard flash, so that change was reverted the
  same day.

### Popover and dropdown: the arrow leaves with the panel

The enter and exit classes now go on the container that holds both the panel and its
arrow. On the panel alone the arrow stayed fully drawn while the panel faded out, then
vanished in one frame when the host was hidden. `getAnimationTarget()` returns that
container, so r-select and r-popover still wait for the right animation.

### `r-token-meter`: counts roll

The readout goes through `rollText` from `ranuts/utils`. When the count changes only the
characters that changed roll in, up from below when it grew, down from above when it shrank.
The text always holds the current value, so the readout reads, copies and announces as before.

### Message API: `stacked`

`message.info({ content, stacked: true })` piles the container's toasts in one place,
newest in front, each older one 10px lower and 5% smaller, more than two behind faded out.
Pointing at the pile or moving focus into it fans the toasts out at their own heights; the
pile closes 200ms after the pointer leaves, so crossing the gap between cards does not shut
it. Like `top`, the option applies to the whole container and is off when left out; the
flat list is unchanged.

### Tests

`test/e2e/motion.spec.ts` covers the press scale and its timing, the rolling readout, and a
pile that fans out and stays open while the pointer walks down it, in Chromium, Firefox and
WebKit. `rollText` has its own unit tests in `packages/ranuts/test/utils/roll-text.test.ts`.

## packages/ranui — `r-glass`: materials, a lensed edge and refraction over images

Studied against reactdrops' glass components. Their glass looks real because it bends light
by the shape of the surface and splits it by wavelength; ours blurred the backdrop and moved
it with random noise. The rework keeps `backdrop-filter`, so the live page behind the glass
still works, and adds a second path for the case where the backdrop is a known image.

### Use

- **`material`** picks every optical value at once: `thin`, `regular` (default), `thick`
  or `clear`. `blur`, `saturate`, `displace`, `bevel`, `dispersion` and `specular` override
  one value each. `GLASS_MATERIALS` exports the table.
- **`backdrop`** refracts an `<img>` or `<video>` with WebGL2: empty for the first one in the
  glass's parent, or a selector. The image stays in the page; the glass paints a refracted
  copy of the part it covers. Without WebGL2, or for a cross-origin image without CORS, the
  pane keeps its CSS glass.
- **`tone`** (`dark` or `light`) says what is behind the glass. Over a `backdrop` it follows
  the image's brightness when unset. Content now takes an ink that reads on the glass
  (`--ran-glass-ink`: white, near-black on a light tone), and the tone in effect is
  `:state(dark)` / `:state(light)`.

### Optics

- The SVG filter's displacement map is drawn from the pane's own shape instead of
  turbulence: flat in the middle, pulling the backdrop inward over a curved edge band
  (`bevel`) the way a thick convex edge magnifies. Red, green and blue are pulled by
  different amounts (`dispersion`). Chromium renders it; other engines keep the frost.
- The WebGL refraction frosts by mip level, bends seven bands of the spectrum each by its
  own amount, and reflects the backdrop just outside the edge into the rim.
- `rim` draws an outer and an inner wall, a key light from the top left and a dim fill from
  the bottom right, thickness that varies along the edge and occasional glints; on a light
  tone it shades the walls instead. WebGL and WebGPU render the same shader.

### Breaking

- `frequency` is gone, with the turbulence it tuned.
- `displace` is now the edge's pull in px (it was the noise displacement scale); the
  defaults come from the material.
- Content inside the glass is white by default (it inherited the page color). Set
  `--ran-glass-ink` to keep a specific color.
- The rim renderer's `setRadius(radius)` is now `set({ radius, bevel, strength, tone })`.

### Foundation tokens

`--ran-backdrop-ink-dark`, `--ran-backdrop-ink-light`, `--ran-backdrop-highlight`,
`--ran-backdrop-edge-light` and `--ran-backdrop-shadow-light` are colors for surfaces over a
backdrop. Like `--ran-media-background`, they follow what is behind the surface, so they are
not redefined in dark mode.

### Documentation site

The glass playground follows reactdrops' control panel: grouped fields with the value inside
each, a material select, switches, three backdrop pictures plus your own upload, and a
snippet that writes only what differs from the material.
